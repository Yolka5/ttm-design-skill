# Read mode

For articles, docs and explainers. The reading column is calm and plain, and it is broken up by images and colour that carry meaning.

## The column

- Use `.prose`: about 38rem wide, 19px text, 1.6 line height.
- Keep paragraphs short. One idea, two or three sentences.
- Bold a sentence or two per section so a skimmer gets the point.
- Let images and diagrams run wider than the text.

## Colour as meaning

This is what lifts an explainer above a blog post. Give each concept a colour and use it everywhere that concept appears: the term in the text, the section band, the cards, the image tint and the sticky label. The reader learns the code without being told.

- Use up to three meaning colours, with `.tone-a`, `.tone-b` and `.tone-c` on a section.
- Use `.tone-band` for a full-width section opener.
- Use `.mark` for a colour-coded term in body text, sparingly. Never in a heading.
- For a concept that combines two others, a gradient between their colours says so.

## Section labels

`.section-label` sticks to the top until the next section takes over, so a reader always knows which part they are in. Use it for long pieces with real chapters, not on short ones.

## Explainer cards

A repeating card for items in a set: art on top, a white label overlapping the art, then a tinted panel with the background and explanation. Cards let a long list stay scannable.

## Link cards

`.link-card` is a tinted "read next" card with an angled image edge. Tone it to match the section. Two or three per article at most.

## Choice preview

`.option` shows what lines up with each choice, like parties that share your answer on a voting quiz. Use it only on pages where people compare options.

## Lessons

For pages that teach, where the reader learns by trying something. Build each step around what they do.

- Keep one order for every step: a short explanation, something to try, the formula or rule, then one question.
- Hide the formula until the reader has tried the thing, with a quiet button to show it anyway so nobody gets stuck.
- When readers tend to believe something wrong, say so and let them test it. Being shown it fail teaches more than being told.
- Use `.tasks` for what to try, and write each one so doing it leads to the idea. A task ticks itself off when it is done, and every task has to be possible to finish.
- Use `.opts` and `.why` for questions. Every option explains itself, the wrong ones most of all.
- Show one step at a time, and let the reader jump to any step. Use `.rail` on wide screens and `.track` on phones. A count like "3 of 7" goes in the track, never above the heading.
- End a topic with a short quiz that points back to the steps worth another look.

## Words

The writing rules in `references/shared/writing.md` apply here most of all: plain, direct, no filler.

## Motion

Sticky labels, colour shifts between sections and one short parallax are enough. No pinned sections, sheet stacks or letter reveals. If an article seems to need them, it is really a Show page, so go back to the brief and ask.
